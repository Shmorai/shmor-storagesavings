Types of vehicle storage

  • Outdoor parking: a marked space in a secure, fenced yard. The most affordable option, best for vehicles built to live outdoors.
  • Covered parking: a roof but open sides. Protects from sun, rain and snow.
  • Indoor units: a fully enclosed drive-up unit. The best protection from weather and theft, often used for classic cars and motorbikes.
  • Specialist storage: some sites focus on caravans, motorhomes, boats or classic cars and offer extras like battery charging or valeting.

Prepare a vehicle for storage

  • Clean it inside and out, and remove food
  • Check tyre pressures, and consider moving it occasionally to avoid flat spots
  • Use a battery maintainer if the site allows, or disconnect the battery
  • Follow the manufacturer’s advice on fuel and fluids
  • Use a breathable cover
  • Remove valuables and documents

Registration, tax and insurance

In the UK, a vehicle that is kept off public roads and isn’t taxed needs a SORN (Statutory Off Road Notification) with the DVLA. In the US, registration and insurance rules for stored vehicles vary by state. Talk to your insurer: some offer “laid-up” or storage cover, but make sure you’re covered for fire and theft while the vehicle is stored.

What to check at the site

  • Security: gated access, CCTV, lighting and staff
  • Size limits for length and height, especially for RVs and boats on trailers
  • Whether you can work on the vehicle on site (most don’t allow it)
  • Access hours for weekend trips
  • Whether the site is approved by your insurer

Vehicle storage: FAQs

Can I store a car in a regular storage unit?
Some facilities allow vehicles in drive-up units, usually 10 × 20 ft or larger. The vehicle often needs to be registered and insured. Check the facility’s rules first.
Do I need a SORN for a stored car in the UK?
If the vehicle isn’t taxed and is kept off public roads, you need to make a SORN with the DVLA. If it’s still taxed, a SORN isn’t needed.
Is indoor or outdoor RV storage better?
Indoor storage protects against sun, weather and theft but costs more and is harder to find for large RVs. Covered parking is a common middle ground.
