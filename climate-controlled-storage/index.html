Climate-controlled storage: is it worth it?

Climate-controlled units keep temperature, and sometimes humidity, within a set range. They usually cost more, so it’s worth knowing when you really need one.

What “climate-controlled” means

Terms vary by provider. Temperature-controlled units are heated and/or cooled to stay within a range. Humidity-controlled units also limit moisture, which matters most for damp-sensitive items. In the UK and much of Europe, many indoor facilities are insulated and ventilated rather than fully climate-controlled. Always ask the provider exactly what they control and to what range.

Items that benefit from climate control

  • Wooden and antique furniture, which can warp or crack
  • Electronics, computers and musical instruments
  • Paper documents, books and photographs
  • Artwork, leather, fabrics and mattresses
  • Wine and some collectibles
  • Medicines and cosmetics (check the facility allows them)

When a standard unit is fine

  • Short stays in a mild season
  • Garden furniture, tools and bikes
  • Plastic storage boxes of clothes or kitchenware
  • Items you’d happily keep in a garage

Protect your things in any unit

  • Use plastic crates or good-quality boxes, not bin bags
  • Keep items off the floor on pallets
  • Leave a gap between items and walls for airflow
  • Cover furniture with breathable dust sheets, not plastic wrap
  • Make sure everything is completely dry before storing

Climate-controlled storage: FAQs

Is climate-controlled storage worth the extra cost?
For valuable or moisture-sensitive items such as wooden furniture, electronics, documents and art, it is often worth it. For tools, garden furniture or plastic boxes, a standard unit is usually enough.
Do I need climate control for long-term storage?
The longer items are stored, the more exposure they have to temperature swings and damp, so climate control becomes more useful for sensitive items over long periods.
Are indoor units climate-controlled?
Not necessarily. Indoor units are protected from the weather but may not be heated, cooled or humidity-controlled. Ask the facility.
